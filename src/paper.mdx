## Abstract

Generative models such as Diffusion and Flow Matching have significantly advanced robotic visuomotor policies by modeling complex and multimodal action distributions. However, their reliance on multi-step sampling introduces substantial inference latency. Existing one-step acceleration methods improve efficiency, but compressing the iterative generation process into a single long-span update may introduce flow-map approximation errors and impair multimodal action generation. Moreover, velocity predictions along the same generation path may exhibit spectral drift, while the learned velocity field may produce inaccurate action predictions under visual perturbations, further degrading the fidelity and reliability of one-step generation. To address these challenges, we propose a high-fidelity one-step generative visuomotor policy framework based on three complementary mechanisms: recursive correction, frequency consistency, and contrastive flow matching. First, **Recursive Consistent Action Flow (RCAF)** constructs corrected long-span velocity targets through recursive teacher estimation to reduce flow-map approximation errors while supporting multimodal action generation. Second, **Dual-Timestep Frequency Consistency (DTFC)** introduces timestep-aware spectral regularization to suppress intra-flow spectral drift and promote consistent temporal action structures. Third, **Contrastive Flow Matching (CFM)** introduces margin-bounded cross-sample velocity regularization to complement positive flow supervision and improve action prediction reliability. All three mechanisms operate during training without increasing one-step inference cost. Extensive experiments on RoboTwin 1.0, RoboTwin 2.0, Adroit, DexArt, ManiFeel, and real-world robot platforms show that our method achieves competitive or superior manipulation performance compared with strong 10-step generative policy baselines while requiring only one function evaluation (1 NFE). On RoboTwin 1.0, our method achieves an average success rate of 58.9% at 1 NFE, outperforming ManiFlow at 10 NFEs (46.1%).
